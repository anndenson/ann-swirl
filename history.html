History of Ice Cream

Description of the Historical Event Image
Ice cream initially began with Roman Emperor Nero, who had sent runners into the mountains for snow, which was then mixed with honey, fruits, and other juices. This was the earliest known version of ice cream. Oh, if Emperor Nero knew all of the wonders to come soon after!
It was only until the 19th century when Nancy Johnson patented the first hand-crank ice cream maker that the dessert was captivating, or rather freezing, the people's easy access and attention! In 1843, her "Artificial Freezer" used ice and salt to allow smoother textures and made ice cream production simpler as well.
In 1851, a dairyman named Jacob Fussel opened the first commerical ice cream factory. Due to the large amounts of cream he obtained in his job as a dairyman, he created the first large-scale commerical ice cream factory in Seven Valleys, Penssylvania. This action significantly lowered the cost of the culinary delight, as well as making it more available for everyone to enjoy!
In 1904, the waffle cone, which is one of my favorite parts of ice cream, was popularized at the St. Louis World's fair. Prior to this event, several individuals had made their own versions of edible ice cream cups, but this revolutionized how ice cream was presented forever. During the fair, when an ice cream vendor ran out of dishes to serve the dessert in, a nearby waffle vendor rolled his pastries into a thin cone to hold the ice cream. This unprecedented action turned out to be a major success (not a meltdown)!
The last major event that helped ice cream become where it is today is when the first continuous-process freezer was perfected in 1926! Clarence Vogt enabled the mass production of this dessert by using electric refrigeration and consistent batches of ice cream to test and innovate the machine that drove the modern ice cream industry.
